# What this project was trying to do:

My broader framework proposes that confidence is not a simple read-out of accumulated evidence, but a third-order evaluation. Representations are evaluated for internal coherence (ist order). These representations are then sampled across contexts, yielding a robustness estimate (2nd order), and confidence is the evaluation of the estimated robustness of the representation (3rd order). This project examines a single outcome of the structure rather than the structure in its entirety. The hypothesis is that if confidence monitors representational coherence rather than solely evidence strength, then interfering with representational coherence by adding conflicting evidence will lead to a greater reduction in confidence compared to a comparable manipulation that just degrades evidence quality, even when the participant’s decision is accurate.

This simulation was built to test whether this specific signature is definable as a separate pathway for evidence-degradation and coherence-conflict, which feeds accuracy and confidence differently. It was also designed to check if it was statistically detectable by a standard hierarchical ordinal model. It was not built to test the generative/regulatory claim in the fuller theory, which is that confidence itself feeds back to regulate decision and action.

In this simulation, as a deliberate simplification, confidence does not influence subsequent evidence accumulation, decision thresholds, or behavior. This project therefore tests a basic, non-regulatory instance of the broader hypothesis. To test the regulative claim, we need a different generative model. This model would incorporate a confidence/robustness estimate back into the decision-making process. This is a planned extension that has not yet been implemented. It also doesn’t yet detail how the “certain contexts” for coherence sampling are chosen or created; this lack of specificity is acknowledged, not fixed, in this text.

# Results
## Hierarchical Bayesian Model Recovers Sensitivity to Uncertainty Structure
To evaluate whether confidence reports retain information about the generative structure of uncertainty beyond task performance, I fitted a hierarchical Bayesian cumulative logit model to simulated ordinal confidence ratings. The model included fixed effects of uncertainty manipulation (stochastic noise vs. coherence conflict), decision accuracy, and their interaction, with subject-specific random intercepts and slopes for each effect. All Markov chain Monte Carlo chains converged successfully (Ȓ = 1.00 for all monitored parameters), with adequate effective sample sizes across parameters, indicating stable posterior estimation.

## Population-Level Effects
Confidence was systematically influenced by the type of uncertainty generating the decision. Relative to coherence-conflict trials, the stochastic noise condition produced higher confidence ratings (β = 0.89, 95% CrI [0.71, 1.07]). This effect remained after accounting for task accuracy, indicating that confidence retained information about the uncertainty manipulation itself rather than simply reflecting response correctness. As expected, decision accuracy exerted a strong positive influence on confidence (β = 1.25, 95% CrI [1.10, 1.41]), consistent with confidence increasing for correct responses. A positive interaction between manipulation type and accuracy was also recovered (β = 0.46, 95% CrI [0.31, 0.60]), indicating that the relationship between accuracy and confidence differed across uncertainty regimes. Thus, uncertainty structure influenced not only baseline confidence but also the mapping between decision accuracy and subjective confidence.

## Individual Differences
The hierarchical model recovered substantial between-subject variability in the effects of the uncertainty manipulation (SD = 0.51) and decision accuracy (SD = 0.41), indicating heterogeneity in confidence calibration across simulated observers. Variability in the manipulation-by-accuracy interaction was comparatively small (SD = 0.11), suggesting that while observers differed in their overall sensitivity to uncertainty type, they showed less variation in how uncertainty altered the relationship between accuracy and confidence. The strong negative correlation between subject-specific intercepts and manipulation slopes (ρ = –0.75) suggests that simulated observers with higher baseline confidence exhibited larger confidence differences between uncertainty conditions. Correlations involving the interaction term were associated with broad credible intervals and were not interpreted further.

# Interpretation
Across simulations, the fitted hierarchical model successfully recovered the qualitative distinction embedded in the generative process. Although the two manipulation conditions were designed to produce comparable task performance, confidence judgments remained systematically sensitive to the underlying source of uncertainty. These simulations demonstrate that, under the proposed generative assumptions, standard hierarchical ordinal models are capable of recovering latent differences in uncertainty structure from confidence reports. This establishes the computational identifiability of the proposed mechanism and motivates future empirical tests using behavioural data. 

# Status
This is an exploratory simulation testing one implication of a larger, still-developing theoretical framework. It is not empirical evidence for the theory, and it deliberately brackets the generative/regulatory claim and the context-sampling mechanism, both of which remain open theoretical questions. It is presented as a demonstration of the formalize → simulate step in the framework's methodology, not as a completed test.
